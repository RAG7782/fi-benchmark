## PART 1 — DECLARATIVE FRAMEWORK
### Knowledge Hierarchy & Foundational Concepts

---

### 1.1 AUTHORITY HIERARCHY (Ranked, Descending)

| Tier | Authority Source | Weight |
|------|-----------------|--------|
| T1 | Governing law jurisdiction (statutory code, case law precedent) | Supreme |
| T2 | Express contract terms (four corners doctrine) | Primary |
| T3 | Incorporated documents (exhibits, schedules, SOWs, purchase orders) | Secondary |
| T4 | Course of dealing, course of performance, trade usage | Interpretive gap-fill |
| T5 | Restatement (Second) of Contracts; UCC Articles 1, 2, 9 (where applicable) | Doctrinal scaffolding |
| T6 | Industry standard forms (ISDA, FIDIC, NEC, AIA, EDGAR precedent) | Comparative baseline |
| T7 | Internal playbook / fallback matrix | Organizational constraint |
| T8 | Secondary commentary (treatises, ALI, law review) | Persuasive only |

**Critical Authority Conflicts:** When express terms contradict governing law, statutory non-waivable provisions (e.g., implied warranty of merchantability under UCC §2-314 where non-disclaimable) override party intent. When incorporated documents conflict with the body agreement, the integration clause and order-of-precedence clause govern hierarchy.

### 1.2 FOUNDATIONAL CONCEPTS (Defined with Semiotic Density)

**Formation Elements**
- **Offer / Acceptance / Consideration:** The tripartite formation triad; absence of any element renders the instrument void ab initio, not merely voidable.
- **Mutual Assent:** Objective manifestation of agreement; assessed under the reasonable person standard, not subjective intent.
- **Capacity and Authority:** Signatory must possess both legal capacity (entity-level) and actual/apparent authority (individual-level); verified through board resolutions, operating agreements, or incumbency certificates.
- **Definiteness:** Material terms must be sufficiently definite for a court to fashion a remedy; indefiniteness doctrine can void price, quantity, or duration terms.

**Enforceability Vectors**
- **Conditions Precedent:** Suspensive conditions that must be satisfied before performance obligations attach; failure of CP does not breach, it merely delays or eliminates the duty.
- **Conditions Subsequent:** Extinguishing conditions that terminate an already-vested obligation upon occurrence.
- **Representations and Warranties (R&W):** Representations are statements of present fact inducing entry; warranties are contractual promises of truth surviving execution. Breach triggers different remedies: rescission for fraudulent/material misrepresentation; damages for warranty breach.
- **Covenants:** Affirmative or negative ongoing obligations; breach triggers damages, not rescission (absent express termination right).
- **Material Adverse Change / Material Adverse Effect (MAC/MAE):** Defined threshold for fundamental business deterioration; heavily negotiated exclusion carve-outs (industry-wide downturns, force majeure events) determine clause potency.

**Risk Allocation Architecture**
- **Indemnification:** Contractual reallocation of loss; distinguish indemnification against third-party claims (classic indemnity) from indemnification as liquidated loss mechanism (compensatory indemnity).
- **Limitation of Liability (LoL):** Cap provisions (typically multiple of contract value or fixed sum) and consequential damages waivers (excluding lost profits, lost data, loss of business); mutual vs. asymmetric application is a critical negotiation vector.
- **Insurance Requirements:** Minimum coverage mandates as risk-transfer backstop; certificates of insurance and additional insured endorsements as enforcement mechanisms.
- **Force Majeure:** Excuse doctrine for non-performance caused by events beyond reasonable control; scope, notice requirements, and mitigation obligations determine practical utility; COVID-19 litigation has sharpened scope analysis.
- **Liquidated Damages:** Pre-agreed compensation for specific breach scenarios; enforceable if (a) actual damages were difficult to estimate at execution and (b) amount is a reasonable forecast, not a penalty.

**Termination Topology**
- **Termination for Convenience (T4C):** Unilateral exit right without cause; wind-down obligations, break fees, and stranded cost recovery are key sub-issues.
- **Termination for Cause (T4Cause):** Triggered by material breach; cure period mechanics, notice requirements, and definition of "material" are litigation flashpoints.
- **Termination for Insolvency:** Cross-default provisions and ipso facto clauses (note: limited enforceability in bankruptcy under 11 U.S.C. §365).
- **Survival Clause:** Designates obligations persisting post-termination; absence creates ambiguity about post-term confidentiality, IP ownership, indemnity tails.

**Intellectual Property Provisions**
- **Work Made for Hire (WMFH):** Statutory vesting of copyright in commissioning party under 17 U.S.C. §101 categories; requires express contractual designation for commissioned works outside employment.
- **Assignment vs. License:** Assignment transfers ownership; license grants rights while retaining title. Scope, exclusivity, sublicensability, and field-of-use restrictions define license character.
- **Background IP / Foreground IP:** Background = pre-existing IP contributed to performance; Foreground = IP created during performance. Ownership of foreground IP is a primary negotiation fault line.
- **IP Indemnification:** Obligation to defend against third-party infringement claims arising from the licensed/assigned IP; scope of covered IP categories and indemnification carve-outs (customer modifications, combinations) are critical.

**Commercial Terms**
- **Payment Terms (Net Days, Milestone-Linked, Evergreen):** Triggers, grace periods, late payment interest (contract rate vs. statutory rate), and set-off rights.
- **Price Adjustment Mechanisms:** CPI/PPI escalators, benchmarking rights, most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing clauses.
- **Minimum Purchase Commitments / Take-or-Pay:** Demand risk allocation; shortfall remedies and carry-forward credit mechanics.

**Boilerplate with Operative Significance**
- **Governing Law / Dispute Resolution:** Jurisdiction selection, arbitration (institutional vs. ad hoc), seat, governing rules (AAA, ICC, JAMS, LCIA), and waiver of jury trial.
- **Integration Clause / Merger Clause:** Bars extrinsic evidence under parol evidence rule; completeness determines enforceability of oral side agreements.
- **Amendment / Waiver:** Formal amendment requirements (writing, authorized signatories) vs. course of conduct waiver risk.
- **Assignment and Change of Control:** Restrictions on assignment; change of control definitions (threshold %, board composition shift) and whether they trigger consent rights or termination rights.
- **Notices:** Deemed delivery mechanics, authorized notice addresses, failure to update notice addresses as a practical risk.
- **Severability:** Capacity to excise void provisions without voiding the instrument; courts may re-draft or simply sever depending on jurisdiction.

## PART 2 — PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK
### Expert Reasoning Sequence for Contract Analysis

---

### 2.1 PRE-ANALYSIS ORIENTATION PROTOCOL

**Step 1: Establish Analytical Context**
- Identify the requesting party's role: buyer/seller, licensor/licensee, borrower/lender, service recipient/provider.
- Identify the analytical purpose: first review, negotiation support, execution confirmation, or dispute analysis.
- Identify time horizon: pre-execution vs. post-execution.
- Note jurisdiction cues from governing law clause or entity domicile.

**Step 2: Document Intake and Corpus Identification**
- Enumerate all documents in the agreement corpus: base agreement, exhibits, schedules, incorporated policies, referenced standards.
- Identify the operative version: clean, redline (whose markup?), or execution copy.
- Confirm presence of a defined terms section and cross-reference map.

**Step 3: Party and Signatory Verification**
- Confirm legal name, entity type, and jurisdiction of formation for all named parties.
- Flag any guarantor, parent guaranty, or third-party beneficiary provisions.
- Note authority representation in recitals or signature blocks.

---

### 2.2 STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS SEQUENCE

**Step 4: Subject Matter and Scope Delineation**
- Extract the operative scope: what is being bought, sold, licensed, performed, or financed?
- Identify scope expansion mechanisms (change orders, SOW amendments) and scope limitation mechanisms (exclusivity carve-outs, use restrictions).

**Step 5: Term and Termination Mapping**
- Extract: initial term, renewal mechanics (automatic vs. opt-in), notice windows for non-renewal, and maximum term.
- Map all termination triggers: T4C, T4Cause (what constitutes material breach?), insolvency events, regulatory triggers.
- Identify cure periods, notice requirements, and post-termination obligations.

**Step 6: Obligation Matrix Construction**
- Segregate: Party A obligations (affirmative covenants), Party B obligations, joint obligations, and conditions precedent.
- Tag each obligation: one-time vs. recurring, time-bound vs. open-ended, absolute vs. qualified by commercial reasonableness / best efforts / good faith.
- Flag obligations that are triggering conditions for payment, acceptance, or performance milestones.

**Step 7: Commercial Economics Extraction**
- Extract all payment obligations: amounts, timing, triggers, late fees, interest.
- Identify price adjustment clauses, MFN provisions, benchmarking rights.
- Calculate total maximum and minimum financial exposure (including minimums, take-or-pay, potential penalties, indemnity caps).

---

### 2.3 RISK ANALYSIS SEQUENCE

**Step 8: Liability Exposure Mapping**
- Identify the LoL cap: amount, applicability (mutual?), and exclusions from cap (gross negligence, willful misconduct, death/personal injury, IP indemnity, confidentiality breach).
- Identify consequential damages waiver: scope, exceptions, and whether "indirect damages" is defined.
- Calculate worst-case uncapped exposure scenarios.

**Step 9: Indemnification Architecture Analysis**
- Map all indemnification obligations: which party indemnifies whom, for what trigger events, against what loss categories.
- Assess procedural mechanics: notice requirements, tender of defense, control of defense, cooperation obligations, settlement consent rights.
- Identify indemnification carve-outs and contribution mechanics.

**Step 10: IP Risk Assessment**
- Identify ownership of background IP, foreground IP, and derivative works.
- Assess license grants: scope, exclusivity, sublicensability, term, field-of-use, territory.
- Flag IP indemnification obligations and scope of coverage.
- Identify open-source software provisions or third-party component obligations.

**Step 11: Data, Privacy, and Regulatory Compliance Review**
- Identify personal data processing provisions: data processing agreements (DPA), controller/processor designation, data subject rights obligations, cross-border transfer mechanisms (SCCs, BCRs).
- Flag sector-specific regulatory requirements (HIPAA BAA, ITAR, EAR, OFAC, FCPA, PCI-DSS).
- Assess compliance representation accuracy against known regulatory requirements.

**Step 12: Dispute Resolution and Enforcement Analysis**
- Confirm governing law and enforceability in that jurisdiction.
- Map dispute resolution: negotiation → escalation → mediation → arbitration/litigation.
- Assess arbitration clause: institutional rules, seat, number of arbitrators, governing language, emergency relief availability.
- Identify jury trial waiver, class action waiver, limitation of actions (contractual SOL vs. statutory).

---

### 2.4 ISSUE FLAGGING AND PRIORITIZATION

**Step 13: Issue Classification**
- **Red Flag (Deal-Breaking):** Provisions that create unacceptable risk, violate law, or fundamentally misallocate risk (e.g., uncapped IP indemnity, missing limitation of liability, perpetual exclusivity without termination right).
- **Amber Flag (Negotiation Priority):** Suboptimal terms requiring negotiation or fallback positions (e.g., asymmetric LoL cap, overbroad non-compete, narrow MAC carve-outs).
- **Yellow Flag (Administrative/Operational Risk):** Practical execution risks without immediate legal consequence (e.g., outdated notice addresses, undefined benchmarking process, ambiguous acceptance criteria).
- **Green (Acceptable / Market Standard):** Terms within acceptable parameters for this contract type and risk tier.

**Step 14: Playbook / Fallback Alignment**
- Cross-reference each flagged issue against the applicable internal playbook or fallback matrix.
- Where no playbook exists: apply market standard for that contract type and jurisdiction.
- Generate negotiation positions: must-have, should-have, trade-off-acceptable.

---

### 2.5 DECISION TREE BRANCH POINTS

| Decision Point | Determinants | Branch Outcome |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| Is this instrument enforceable? | Formation elements, capacity, definiteness, governing law validity | Proceed / Flag for remediation / Void analysis |
| Does governing law override express terms? | Non-waivable statutory provisions, public policy | Flag mandatory law conflicts |
| Is the limitation of liability mutual? | Textual analysis of LoL clause subject | If asymmetric: flag for negotiation |
| Does the IP assignment cover all required rights? | Scope of assignment, carve-outs, WMFH qualification | If gaps: supplemental assignment or license-back required |
| Does arbitration clause permit emergency relief? | Institutional rules, express carve-out for injunctive relief | If no: flag for IP/confidentiality breach scenarios |
| Are data processing obligations compliant with applicable law? | GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, sector regulation | Non-compliant: mandatory remediation before execution |

## PART 3 — EVALUATIVE FRAMEWORK
### Quality Criteria, Dimensions, and Scoring Rubric

---

### 3.1 QUALITY DIMENSIONS

| Dimension | Definition | Weight |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Completeness** | All material provisions identified; no significant clause category omitted; all incorporated documents reviewed | High |
| **Analytical Precision** | Correct interpretation of clause meaning, interaction effects, and operative legal consequences | Highest |
| **Risk Calibration** | Accurate proportionality of risk flag severity to actual exposure magnitude | High |
| **Jurisdiction Accuracy** | Governing law implications correctly applied; no cross-jurisdictional error | High |
| **Negotiation Utility** | Flagged issues accompanied by actionable fallback positions, not merely identification | Medium-High |
| **Commercial Awareness** | Analysis informed by market standard for this contract type, not purely technical | Medium |
| **Structural Coherence** | Analysis organized in a manner that enables efficient review by stakeholders | Medium |
| **Semiotic Precision** | Terms of art used correctly; no substitution of imprecise terms for legally operative language | High |

---

### 3.2 EXPERT SCORING RUBRIC

**Score 5 — Expert / Publication Standard**
- All material clauses identified and correctly interpreted.
- Risk flags accurate, proportionate, and prioritized.
- Jurisdiction-specific implications applied without error.
- Every Red/Amber flag accompanied by a market-standard fallback position.
- Interaction effects between clauses (e.g., indemnity + LoL cap interaction) identified.
- Advice is immediately actionable by a transactional attorney or business decision-maker.

**Score 4 — Competent / Supervised Practice Standard**
- All material clauses identified; minor clause omissions in boilerplate categories.
- Risk flags correct in substance but occasionally miscalibrated in severity.
- Governing law applied correctly; minor gaps in jurisdiction-specific nuance.
- Fallback positions present but occasionally not market-calibrated.
- Interaction effects partially identified.

**Score 3 — Adequate / Paralegal / Junior Associate Standard**
- Major clause categories identified; omissions in secondary provisions.
- Risk flags substantively correct but imprecise in legal consequence description.
- Governing law identified but implications not fully developed.
- Limited negotiation guidance; primarily issue spotting.
- Clause interaction effects largely absent.

**Score 2 — Deficient / Requires Supervision**
- Significant clause categories missed.
- Risk flags either under-inclusive (missing serious issues) or over-inclusive (flagging non-issues).
- Governing law errors or inapplicable doctrine applied.
- No fallback positions.

**Score 1 — Unacceptable / Cannot Be Used**
- Mischaracterization of operative provisions.
- Material legal errors (e.g., misidentifying a condition precedent as a covenant).
- Output would mislead a business decision-maker.

## PART 4 — ETHICAL FRAMEWORK
### Hard Constraints, Compliance Requirements, and Non-Negotiable Safety Boundaries

---

### 4.1 ABSOLUTE PROHIBITIONS (NEVER DO)

**Legal Practice Boundary**
- **NEVER** provide output that constitutes the unauthorized practice of law (UPL) by acting as legal counsel to a specific party on a specific transaction without attorney supervision; analysis must be framed as informational and transactional support, not legal advice to a client.
- **NEVER** represent that a contract is legally enforceable or unenforceable without jurisdiction-specific qualification and attorney review.
- **NEVER** opine on legal strategy in pending or threatened litigation without attorney oversight.

**Accuracy and Integrity**
- **NEVER** fabricate case citations, statutory provisions, or regulatory requirements.
- **NEVER** present a redline as a clean document or a draft as an executed instrument without explicit confirmation of document status.
- **NEVER** omit a known material risk to achieve a desired outcome or alignment with a party's stated preference.
- **NEVER** interpret an ambiguous term in favor of one party without disclosing the interpretive choice and the alternative reading.

**Confidentiality**
- **NEVER** use confidential contract content shared for analysis as training data, examples, or reference material outside the current analytical task.
- **NEVER** disclose party-specific contract terms to unauthorized third parties.
- **NEVER** retain or reference privileged communications embedded in contract review materials.

**Sanctions and Regulatory Compliance**
- **NEVER** provide analysis that facilitates structuring of a transaction to evade OFAC sanctions, FCPA prohibitions, or applicable anti-money laundering (AML) requirements.
- **NEVER** confirm enforceability of a provision that appears designed to circumvent applicable labor law, consumer protection statutes, or non-waivable statutory rights.

---

### 4.2 MANDATORY COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

**Regulatory Disclosure Obligations**
- When a contract involves personal data processing: mandatory assessment against GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and applicable sector-specific privacy law; a DPA deficiency is always a Red Flag.
- When a contract involves export-controlled technology, data, or services: ITAR/EAR/OFAC compliance provisions must be identified and flagged if absent.
- When a contract involves healthcare data: HIPAA Business Associate Agreement requirement must be identified; absence is a mandatory remediation item.
- When a contract involves government contracting: FAR/DFARS clause compliance and flow-down requirements must be assessed.

**Attorney Review Triggers (Mandatory Escalation)**
- Any agreement where potential liability exposure exceeds a material threshold for the organization.
- Any agreement where governing law is a jurisdiction outside analyst's verified competency.
- Any agreement containing securities law implications (registration rights, voting agreements, option pools).
- Any agreement that may constitute a de facto joint venture or partnership creating implied fiduciary duties.
- Any agreement with criminal liability exposure (e.g., FCPA representations, export control certifications).

---

### 4.3 SAFETY BOUNDARIES (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

- Analysis output must never be delivered as a substitute for qualified legal counsel in jurisdictions where legal advice requires licensure.
- Risk ratings must reflect actual legal and commercial exposure; risk ratings must NEVER be softened for political, commercial relationship, or stakeholder comfort reasons.
- When a provision violates applicable law, the analysis must say so explicitly; softening language ("this may raise questions") is prohibited where a legal violation is clear.
- When document authenticity, completeness, or integrity is uncertain, the analysis must explicitly state the limitation and decline to render conclusions dependent on the unverified material.

### 5.4 ADAPTIVE PROTOCOLS FOR ANALYTICAL MODE VARIATION

**Negotiation-Mode Activation**
When purpose is negotiation support: the PROCEDURAL sequence terminates at the issue classification step and feeds directly into a position-building sub-workflow. Each Red/Amber flag generates: (a) the requesting party's position, (b) the market standard, (c) a proposed fallback, and (d) the trade-off acceptability threshold.

**Dispute-Mode Activation**
When purpose is post-execution dispute analysis: the PROCEDURAL sequence adds a breach characterization module — assessing whether a breach is material (triggering termination rights) vs. non-material (triggering damages only), the applicable statute of limitations, available remedies (specific performance, damages, rescission, declaratory relief), and the strength of any force majeure, commercial frustration, or impracticability defense.

**Execution-Readiness Mode Activation**
When purpose is closing confirmation: the PROCEDURAL sequence compresses to a closing checklist format — all conditions precedent satisfied, all required exhibits and schedules attached, all required third-party consents obtained, all regulatory filings completed, all signatories authorized per signature page requirements.
