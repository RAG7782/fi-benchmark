### 1.2 Foundational Concepts (High-Semiotic-Density Definitions)

**Pretest Probability (PTP):** The clinician-estimated prevalence of a condition in the specific patient population *before* any diagnostic test is applied; the Bayesian prior anchoring all subsequent likelihood ratio modifications. PTP is context-sensitive: altered by patient demographics, chief complaint prevalence, epidemiological exposure, and clinical setting (ED vs. primary care vs. ICU).

**Likelihood Ratio (LR+/LR−):** The multiplicative factor by which a positive or negative test result shifts the post-test odds of disease. LR+ >10 or LR− <0.1 represent diagnostically decisive thresholds; LR between 2–5 shift probability meaningfully but rarely confirm or exclude alone.

**Posterior Probability:** Post-test probability derived by applying the likelihood ratio to pretest odds (not pretest probability) via Fagan nomogram or Bayes theorem. The operative clinical probability that drives diagnostic and therapeutic decisions.

**Pathognomonic Sign:** A clinical finding with a positive likelihood ratio approaching infinity — presence alone sufficient to establish diagnosis. Rare in practice; examples include Koplik spots (measles), Negri bodies (rabies), opsoclonus-myoclonus (paraneoplastic/neuroblastoma context).

**Illness Script:** A cognitively organized prototype schema encoding: epidemiological risk factors → pathophysiological mechanism → clinical course → key discriminating features → outcome trajectory. Expert diagnosticians activate illness scripts as pattern-completion engines against incoming patient data.

**Pivot Pivot Feature (Discriminating Feature):** A clinical finding, sign, symptom, or test result that specifically bifurcates two competing diagnoses on the differential — present in one, absent in the other — thereby functioning as a diagnostic fulcrum. Identified by computing LR+ and LR− for that feature across competing diagnoses.

**Anchoring Bias:** The cognitive distortion in which the first salient diagnosis activated in working memory receives disproportionate weight, suppressing ongoing Bayesian revision as new contradictory data arrive. Recognized as a principal cause of diagnostic error.

**Premature Closure:** The cognitive error of ceasing diagnostic search once a plausible diagnosis is established, without adequately excluding competing high-stakes diagnoses. Operationally distinct from anchoring: anchoring is attentional; premature closure is behavioral.

**Occam's Razor vs. Hickam's Dictum:** Parsimony principle (Occam) — prefer the single unifying diagnosis. Hickam's counter-principle — patients may have as many diagnoses as they please. Expert clinicians apply Occam preferentially in young, otherwise healthy patients; Hickam's in elderly, multimorbid, and immunocompromised patients where coincident pathology is statistically expected.

**Diagnostic Threshold:** The posterior probability above which treatment is initiated without further testing (treatment threshold) and below which a condition is excluded from active consideration (test threshold). Thresholds are disease-specific, modulated by treatability, morbidity of missed diagnosis, and test invasiveness.

**Zebra Principle:** Medical education heuristic — "When you hear hoofbeats, think horses, not zebras" — operationalizing base-rate-first reasoning. Counterbalanced by: certain settings (tropical medicine, rare disease clinics, tertiary referral) where zebra prevalence increases sufficiently to alter PTP.

**Pertinent Negative:** A clinical finding whose *absence* carries a significant positive LR— for a diagnostic category; a deliberately elicited negative that actively constrains the differential. Distinct from undocumented absence.

### 2.2 Phase-by-Phase Operational Detail

#### PHASE 1: Chief Complaint Activation

- Extract **anchor symptom** (the dominant presenting complaint).
- Identify **symptom characterization dimensions**: onset (sudden/gradual), quality, location, radiation, severity, temporal pattern (constant/intermittent/progressive), modifying factors, associated symptoms.
- Apply the **OPQRST-A framework** (Onset, Provocation/Palliation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Time course, Associated symptoms) as structured elicitation protocol.
- Activate **epidemiological priors**: age, sex, comorbidities, medications, allergies, family history, social history (occupation, travel, exposures, sexual history, substance use).
- **Decision Point 1:** Is this a time-critical presentation requiring *simultaneous diagnosis and resuscitation*?
  - YES → Activate **treat-while-test** protocol; don't delay empirical treatment for diagnostic certainty.
  - NO → Proceed to sequential diagnostic reasoning.

#### PHASE 2: Illness Script Pattern Matching

- Cross-reference the activated symptom complex against stored illness scripts.
- Identify the **semantic prototype match** — which illness script achieves the highest feature-overlap with the presenting data.
- Identify **schema violations** — data points that *don't* fit the prototype match. These are diagnostically informative.
- Activate **competing illness scripts** for all prototypes achieving partial match.
- **Decision Point 2:** Does the presentation achieve *high-fidelity* match to a single illness script?
  - YES → Proceed with that script as anchor; widen search only for must-not-miss items.
  - NO → Expand to full differential hypothesis generation.

#### PHASE 3: Differential Hypothesis Generation

- Generate an **unanchored differential** using VINDICATE taxonomy: enumerate all mechanistically plausible diagnoses.
- Classify each candidate diagnosis into the **prioritization matrix**:
  - **Category A (Must-Not-Miss):** Diagnoses where delay = catastrophic outcome (PE, ACS, aortic dissection, meningitis, stroke, ectopic pregnancy, sepsis, malignancy).
  - **Category B (High-Probability):** Diagnoses consistent with base rates in presenting context.
  - **Category C (Treatable-First):** Diagnoses where empirical treatment is low-risk and diagnostically informative.
  - **Category D (Unifying Diagnosis):** The most parsimonious explanation.
- Apply **Occam vs. Hickam adjudication** based on patient multimorbidity status.
- **Minimum differential breadth:** 3 diagnoses; **standard breadth:** 5–8; **complex/undifferentiated cases:** >10.

#### PHASE 4: Discriminating Feature Elicitation

- For each pair of competing high-probability and must-not-miss diagnoses, identify the **pivoting discriminating feature**.
- Structure elicitation as a **directed hypothesis-testing history**: each question aimed at raising or lowering posterior probability of specific diagnoses, not confirmatory bias-driven questioning.
- Conduct **systems-based physical examination** with deliberate focus on findings that discriminate between leading hypotheses.
- Document **pertinent negatives explicitly** — their absence carries informational weight.
- **Decision Point 3:** Does the discriminating feature pattern uniquely resolve the differential?
  - YES → Proceed to threshold determination.
  - NO → Formulate a testing strategy to acquire missing discriminating data.

#### PHASE 5: Bayesian Iterative Revision

- For each diagnostic test ordered or result received:
  1. State the **pretest probability** of target condition.
  2. Identify the **likelihood ratio** (LR+ or LR−) for that test result.
  3. Convert pretest probability → pretest odds → apply LR → calculate posterior odds → convert to **posterior probability**.
  4. Update the active differential ranking.
- Apply **sequential Bayesian revision** — each new finding revises the prior established by the previous finding (assuming conditional independence, or adjust if findings are pathophysiologically correlated).
- Flag **disconfirming data** — findings that reduce probability of the leading diagnosis. These must not be discounted; they are active signals, not noise.
- **Decision Point 4:** Does any single finding produce a posterior probability crossing a diagnostic threshold (treatment or exclusion)?
  - YES → Proceed to threshold declaration.
  - NO → Continue targeted data acquisition.

#### PHASE 6: Diagnostic Threshold Determination

- For the working diagnosis, determine:
  - **Test threshold** (below which diagnosis is excluded without further testing; typically <5% posterior probability for common diagnoses, but disease-specific — e.g., <1–2% for PE in low-risk PERC-negative patients).
  - **Treatment threshold** (above which empirical treatment begins without confirmatory testing; typically >90% for common diagnoses, but modulated by treatment risk/benefit ratio).
  - **Test-treatment zone** (between thresholds; further testing is indicated).
- Apply **decision analysis** when treatment risk is high or diagnosis uncertainty is substantial: formally weight probability × magnitude of harm × treatability.
- **Decision Point 5:** Is the posterior probability in the test-treatment zone?
  - YES → Identify the single test that most efficiently shifts probability across a threshold (greatest Bayesian yield per risk/cost).
  - NO → Act on established threshold.

#### PHASE 7: Testing Strategy Formulation

- Select tests hierarchically:
  1. **Non-invasive, high-sensitivity screening tests** first for must-not-miss diagnoses.
  2. **Confirmatory tests** (high specificity) for diagnoses achieving threshold for treatment consideration.
  3. **Gold-standard tests** only when intermediate tests are non-diagnostic and clinical stakes are high.
- Sequence tests to exploit **conditional probability dependencies** — order tests whose results optimally constrain the prior for the next test.
- Apply **pretest probability gatekeeping**: do not order tests where PTP is below the test threshold *unless* the LR− of the test is sufficient to formally exclude the diagnosis (e.g., D-dimer only in low-probability PE, not high-probability).
- Avoid **defensive testing**: testing that serves medicolegal rather than diagnostic purposes without altering management.

#### PHASE 8: Synthesis and Working Diagnosis Declaration

- Declare a **working diagnosis** — the highest posterior-probability diagnosis consistent with the full clinical dataset.
- Document **competing diagnoses** still requiring monitoring or follow-up data.
- Define **diagnostic criteria satisfaction**: specify which validated criteria (e.g., Duke criteria for IE, Rome criteria for IBS, McDonald criteria for MS) are met or partially met.
- Distinguish between:
  - **Definitive diagnosis**: Gold-standard confirmed.
  - **Probable diagnosis**: Clinical criteria met, gold-standard pending or deferred.
  - **Possible diagnosis**: Partial criteria met; competing diagnoses not excluded.
  - **Syndrome diagnosis**: Mechanistic category identified; specific etiology undetermined.

#### PHASE 9: Diagnostic Safety-Netting

- Explicitly address remaining **diagnostic uncertainty**.
- Define **clinical red flags** that would trigger urgent reassessment and differential expansion.
- Establish **follow-up decision points**: timeline and conditions under which the working diagnosis should be reconsidered.
- Communicate uncertainty explicitly to patient and clinical team — safety-netting is a patient safety obligation, not a hedge.

### 3.2 Expert Scoring Rubric

#### DIFFERENTIAL COMPLETENESS SCORE (DCS)

```
5 — All must-not-miss diagnoses present; complete mechanistic coverage across VINDICATE;
     rare but contextually plausible diagnoses included
4 — All must-not-miss diagnoses present; minor omissions in lower-probability categories
3 — Must-not-miss diagnoses present; meaningful gaps in mechanistic coverage
2 — One or more must-not-miss diagnoses absent; differential skewed by anchoring
1 — Differential is narrow, anchored, mechanism-limited, or epidemiologically implausible
```

#### PROBABILISTIC CALIBRATION SCORE (PCS)

```
5 — Pretest probabilities quantitatively consistent with epidemiological literature;
     LR applications mathematically correct; posterior probabilities defensible
4 — Probabilities directionally correct; minor quantitative imprecision
3 — Ranking order correct; probability estimates show systematic over/underestimation
2 — Ranking partially correct; probabilities poorly calibrated to clinical context
1 — Probabilities reflect availability bias or illness script activation errors; ranking unreliable
```

#### REASONING INTEGRITY SCORE (RIS)

```
5 — Bayesian chain unbroken; disconfirming data addressed; cognitive biases named and
     corrected; diagnostic threshold logic explicit and disease-appropriate
4 — Reasoning coherent; one minor logical gap or uncorrected heuristic shortcut
3 — Reasoning partially coherent; premature closure evident at one decision point
2 — Multiple reasoning gaps; anchoring distortion evident; disconfirming data ignored
1 — Reasoning non-sequential; confirmation bias dominant; threshold logic absent
```

#### COMPOSITE DIAGNOSTIC QUALITY SCORE (CDQS)

```
CDQS = (DCS × 0.35) + (PCS × 0.35) + (RIS × 0.30)

Score Interpretation:
4.5 – 5.0 → Attending/Fellowship level; publishable case analysis quality
3.5 – 4.4 → Senior resident level; safe and sound; minor refinement needed
2.5 – 3.4 → Junior resident level; supervision required; significant gaps present
1.5 – 2.4 → Medical student level; structural errors requiring intervention
<1.5      → Pre-clinical reasoning; systematic recalibration required
```

## SECTION 4: ETHICAL FRAMEWORK — Hard Constraints and Safety Boundaries

### 4.1 ABSOLUTE PROHIBITIONS (Non-Negotiable Constraints)

**CONSTRAINT 1 — Diagnostic Nihilism Prohibition**
*Never* abandon structured differential diagnosis on the basis that a presentation is "atypical" or "complex." Atypicality is a data point that expands, not contracts, the differential. Particularly applies to presentations in women, elderly patients, diabetics, and immunocompromised patients where classical symptom patterns are physiologically suppressed.

**CONSTRAINT 2 — Premature Closure on Partial Data**
*Never* declare a definitive diagnosis and terminate diagnostic reasoning when confirmatory testing has not been obtained and competing must-not-miss diagnoses have not been formally excluded. The working diagnosis is always provisional until appropriate criteria are satisfied.

**CONSTRAINT 3 — Confirmation Bias Testing**
*Never* order diagnostic tests solely to confirm a pre-committed hypothesis while systematically ignoring or deferring tests that could disconfirm it. Diagnostic testing must be bidirectional — designed to both raise and lower the probability of diagnoses.

**CONSTRAINT 4 — Base Rate Neglect**
*Never* assign clinical significance to test results without applying pretest probability. A positive test in a low-prevalence population produces a low posterior probability despite a high LR+. Failing to apply this principle converts the diagnostic process into a false positive generator.

**CONSTRAINT 5 — Diagnostic Attribution Bias**
*Never* allow patient demographic characteristics (age, sex, race, socioeconomic status, psychiatric history, substance use history) to systematically suppress the generation of organic diagnoses. "Frequent flyer," "anxious patient," or "drug-seeking" attribution must not truncate the differential.

**CONSTRAINT 6 — Must-Not-Miss Omission**
*Never* generate a differential for any acute presentation without explicitly addressing and formally excluding (by probability or testing) the life-threatening diagnoses in that symptom category. Chest pain differentials must address ACS, PE, and aortic dissection. Headache differentials must address subarachnoid hemorrhage. Back pain differentials must address aortic aneurysm and epidural abscess in appropriate risk contexts.

**CONSTRAINT 7 — Diagnostic Overreach**
*Never* declare a definitive diagnosis outside the scope of available clinical data. Distinguish clearly between definitive, probable, possible, and syndrome-level diagnoses. Epistemic honesty about diagnostic certainty is a patient safety imperative.

### 4.2 Compliance Requirements

**Evidence Currency:** Diagnostic accuracy data (sensitivity, specificity, LR+/LR−) cited must be sourced from peer-reviewed literature within appropriate recency standards. Sensitivity/specificity estimates from pre-2000 studies should be flagged for potential supersession by contemporary meta-analyses.

**Validated Decision Rules:** Clinical decision rules (Wells score, PERC, HEART, CURB-65, Ottawa rules, etc.) must be applied only within their validated patient populations and clinical settings. Extrapolation outside validation cohort demographics must be explicitly flagged.

**Diagnostic Criteria Fidelity:** Disease-specific diagnostic criteria (DSM-5, ACR criteria, Duke criteria, etc.) must be applied with fidelity to their published formulations. Casual paraphrase that omits required versus supporting criteria is a compliance violation.

**Uncertainty Communication:** All probabilistic statements must be qualified with an explicit acknowledgment of their epistemic status. "The diagnosis is X" without qualification is not permissible when confirmatory testing is outstanding.

---

### 4.3 Safety Boundaries

**Triage Override:** Diagnostic reasoning that delays resuscitation or time-sensitive treatment (tPA in stroke, antibiotics in septic meningitis, PCI in STEMI) is categorically impermissible. Time-critical diagnoses operate under treat-while-test protocols.

**Scope of Practice Boundary:** This framework generates diagnostic reasoning and hypothesis frameworks. It does not replace clinical examination, does not access real-time patient data independently, and does not constitute a clinical decision. All outputs require adjudication by a licensed clinical practitioner with direct patient access.

**Iatrogenic Harm from Overtesting:** Diagnostic pathways that expose patients to cumulative radiation (CT series), procedural risk (invasive testing), or contrast nephropathy without commensurate Bayesian yield represent an ethical constraint violation. Testing economy is both a quality and safety standard.

### 5.2 Inter-Layer Interaction Rules

**Declarative → Procedural Interface:**
The illness script library and VINDICATE taxonomy provide the *inventory* from which procedural reasoning draws. Procedural phases do not generate diagnoses ex nihilo — they activate, rank, and revise entries from the declarative knowledge base. If a diagnosis emerges from procedural reasoning that lacks declarative grounding (illness script, mechanistic pathway), it must be flagged as speculative.

**Procedural → Evaluative Interface:**
Each procedural phase generates an evaluable output: Phase 3 generates a differential list (evaluated by DCS), Phase 5 generates a Bayesian revision chain (evaluated by PCS), Phase 8 generates a diagnostic synthesis (evaluated by RIS). Evaluation is not post-hoc — it is embedded at each phase transition.

**Ethical → All Layers (Constraint Enforcement):**
The ethical layer operates as a continuous constraint on all other layers:
- **Declarative:** Knowledge sources must be evidence-ranked; no appeal to authority below T4 without flagging.
- **Procedural:** At each decision point, the must-not-miss audit runs automatically. Diagnostic attribution bias check runs at Phase 3.
- **Evaluative:** Scoring rubric cannot award high scores to outputs that contain constraint violations even if technically complete.

**Evaluative → Procedural Feedback Loop:**
If the CDQS score at output falls below 3.5, the framework triggers a **diagnostic iteration loop**: return to Phase 3, expand differential using unexplored VINDICATE categories, re-run Bayesian revision with attention to previously discounted disconfirming data, and re-evaluate.

### 5.3 Validation Steps

#### VALIDATION GATE 1 — Differential Completeness Audit (Post-Phase 3)
- **Check:** Are all must-not-miss diagnoses for the symptom complex present?
- **Check:** Does the differential span at least 3 mechanistic categories from VINDICATE?
- **Check:** Has patient demographic-based suppression been excluded (attribution bias audit)?
- **Fail Condition:** Any must-not-miss diagnosis absent → mandatory expansion before proceeding.

#### VALIDATION GATE 2 — Bayesian Coherence Check (Post-Phase 5)
- **Check:** Is each PTP estimate grounded in epidemiological literature or validated clinical decision rule?
- **Check:** Are LR+ and LR− values sourced and plausible for the tests applied?
- **Check:** Is the posterior probability calculation arithmetically consistent (pretest odds × LR = post-test odds)?
- **Check:** Have disconfirming findings been incorporated rather than discounted?
- **Fail Condition:** Arithmetically inconsistent probability chain → recompute before proceeding.

#### VALIDATION GATE 3 — Threshold Logic Audit (Post-Phase 6)
- **Check:** Are test and treatment thresholds disease-specific (not generic)?
- **Check:** Does the threshold calibration account for treatability, morbidity of missed diagnosis, and test risk?
- **Check:** Is the decision to test, treat, or exclude logically consistent with the stated posterior probability relative to the stated thresholds?
- **Fail Condition:** Threshold logic inconsistency (e.g., treating when posterior < treatment threshold without justification) → mandatory decision analysis before proceeding.

#### VALIDATION GATE 4 — Safety-Net Completeness Audit (Post-Phase 9)
- **Check:** Has each remaining diagnostic competitor with catastrophic miss-potential been explicitly acknowledged?
- **Check:** Are clinical red flags for deterioration defined?
- **Check:** Are follow-up criteria and timelines specified?
- **Fail Condition:** Safety-netting absent or incomplete → mandatory addition before output delivery.
