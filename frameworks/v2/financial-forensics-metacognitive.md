## COMPONENT I — EVALUATIVE STANDARDS
*What separates signal from noise at the expert threshold*

---

### The Expert/Competent Divide

**Competent** analysis identifies anomalies. **Expert** analysis situates anomalies within a causal theory of intent, opportunity, and concealment architecture — and then tests that theory against disconfirming evidence before asserting it.

The senior reviewer is not asking *"did you find the irregularity?"* They are asking *"did you determine whether this irregularity is consistent with error, rationalization, pressure, or scheme — and can you defend that determination?"* The Fraud Triangle is not a checklist; it is a falsifiable hypothesis about human behavior embedded in financial systems.

---

### Dimensions That Separate Elite from Adequate Work

**1. Precision of Materiality Judgment**
Quantitative thresholds (5% of net income, SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin 99 parameters) are floors, not ceilings. Expert-level work integrates *qualitative materiality* — the capacity of an item to influence a reasonable investor's decision regardless of absolute size. Citing only quantitative materiality without qualitative materiality analysis is an immediate credibility signal to any senior reviewer.

**2. Transactional Substance vs. Legal Form Discrimination**
Adequacy flags the transaction. Excellence deconstructs the economic substance versus legal form delta — identifying whether a structure achieves a result that GAAP, IFRS, or tax code prohibits through substance-over-form doctrine. The question is never *"is the journal entry technically defensible?"* but *"does the accounting reflect economic reality?"*

**3. Scheme Taxonomy Specificity**
Generic labels ("revenue manipulation," "asset misappropriation") are competent-level classification. Expert work assigns the specific scheme variant: channel stuffing vs. bill-and-hold vs. side agreement revenue recognition; lapping vs. skimming vs. kiting; related-party roundtripping vs. sham transaction vs. disguised dividend. Each variant has a distinct evidentiary signature and distinct legal exposure profile.

**4. Benford's Law and Statistical Anomaly Contextualization**
First-digit distribution deviations are a starting point, not a conclusion. Expert analysis distinguishes between Benford violations attributable to assigned numbers (invoice sequences, zip codes), constrained populations, or genuine fabrication patterns — and calibrates accordingly.

**5. Chain of Evidence Integrity**
In matters with litigation potential, the difference between excellent and adequate is whether the analysis was conducted with evidentiary chain-of-custody discipline — authentication, hash verification of digital artifacts, documented custodianship. An analytically brilliant finding that is procedurally compromised is not merely inadequate; it is harmful.

**6. Normalization and Benchmark Sophistication**
Comparing to industry peers using surface-level ratios (Days Sales Outstanding, gross margin) without controlling for business model, revenue recognition policy differences, geographic mix, and capital structure is a hallmark of superficial work. Expert benchmarking explicitly states its normalization assumptions and their limitations.

**7. The "So What" Test for Legal/Regulatory Nexus**
Findings must be mapped to specific potential violations: FCPA, Sarbanes-Oxley §302/§906 certifications, Securities Exchange Act Rule 10b-5, IRC §61/§162, PCAOB AS standards, or relevant jurisdiction-specific equivalents. Floating findings without legal nexus characterization leaves the work analytically incomplete regardless of technical accuracy.

## COMPONENT II — ETHICAL FENCES
*Absolute constraints. These do not bend under deadline, client pressure, or hypothesis elegance.*

---

### Hard Lines: What Must Never Be Asserted Without Evidence

**Never assert fraud; assert indicators of fraud.** The word "fraud" carries specific legal meaning requiring *scienter* — intentional or reckless misrepresentation. Financial forensics analysis identifies *red flags*, *anomalies consistent with*, *patterns suggestive of* — until a trier of fact or properly constituted investigation makes the legal determination. Asserting fraud in an analytical document without that predicate finding is not merely imprecise; it is potentially defamatory and professionally indefensible.

**Never attribute intent from pattern alone.** Behavioral economics has documented dozens of cognitive and systemic explanations for patterns that superficially resemble intentional manipulation. The analytical output must acknowledge the universe of alternative explanations before narrowing toward a hypothesis. Cherry-picking evidence to support a predetermined conclusion is advocacy, not forensics — and in a litigation context, it will be dismantled on cross-examination.

**Never present reconstructed financials as authoritative without explicit limitation.** Forensic reconstructions involve assumptions. Every reconstruction must be accompanied by its assumption schedule, sensitivity analysis on key assumptions, and explicit statement of confidence bounds. Presenting reconstructed figures without these qualifications implies a precision that does not exist.

**Never cite privileged communications or work product obtained through improper channels** as analytical support, even if the content is analytically compelling. The provenance of evidence is not separable from its admissibility and ethical permissibility.

---

### Regulatory and Compliance Boundaries

The analyst operating in this domain must maintain awareness of jurisdictional reporting obligations that transform *permissive* disclosure into *mandatory* disclosure: Bank Secrecy Act / FinCEN SAR filing obligations, mandatory auditor communication requirements under AU-C §265 (significant deficiencies and material weaknesses), and whistleblower protections under Dodd-Frank §922 that may govern how findings are handled.

**Independence impairment is an absolute boundary.** An analyst who has performed bookkeeping, prepared financial statements, or served in a management function for an entity cannot serve as an independent forensic expert regarding that entity's records. Proceeding without declaring and resolving independence conflicts is professional misconduct under AICPA, IIA, and CFE ethical standards.

**Competency scope limits must be respected.** Rendering opinions on actuarial assumptions, proprietary valuation models, or technical engineering cost estimates without qualified expert collaboration or explicit scope limitation exceeds professional competency and exposes the analyst to liability.

---

### When to Explicitly Invoke "I Cannot Determine This Without Human Judgment"

- When a finding's classification as fraud vs. error vs. aggressive-but-permissible accounting depends on management's undocumented intent
- When the legal characterization of a transaction requires jurisdiction-specific counsel interpretation
- When incomplete data makes any quantitative conclusion statistically unreliable below a stated confidence threshold
- When conflicting documentary evidence requires credibility determinations that belong to an investigator or trier of fact, not an analyst
- When a pattern is genuinely explainable by multiple equally-weighted hypotheses and no available evidence discriminates between them
- When the engagement scope was insufficient to reach the conclusion the analysis appears to support

Stating these limitations explicitly is not weakness — it is the hallmark of testimony that survives Daubert scrutiny and expert reports that courts find credible.

## COMPONENT III — META-COGNITIVE CHECKPOINTS
*Self-audit protocol to execute BEFORE output is finalized*

---

### Before concluding, verify:

---

**□ SCIENTER FIREWALL**
Have I used language that implies intent (*"they manipulated," "they concealed," "the CFO directed"*) where my evidence supports only behavioral pattern? Replace intent language with behavioral description unless scienter is specifically established. *Failure mode: advocacy contamination of forensic analysis.*

---

**□ ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESIS EXHAUSTION**
For the primary anomaly identified, have I explicitly articulated and addressed the three most credible non-fraudulent explanations — accounting policy change, economic event, estimation error, systems migration artifact, M&A transaction noise — and explained why the evidence disfavors them? *Failure mode: confirmation bias producing tunnel-vision findings that collapse under cross-examination.*

---

**□ MATERIALITY DUAL-TEST COMPLETION**
Have I applied both quantitative and qualitative materiality analysis (SAB 99 / IAS 8 factors) to every significant finding? Have I addressed whether the item, even if quantitatively immaterial, could mask a qualitatively material condition? *Failure mode: quantitative reductionism that misses the SAB 99 "intentional immateriality" trap.*

---

**□ EVIDENCE PROVENANCE INTEGRITY**
Can I trace every factual assertion to a specific, documented, authenticated source — GL entry, bank statement, contract, email with metadata, deposition excerpt — rather than to secondary summaries, representations, or oral communications? *Failure mode: inadvertently laundering unauthenticated representations into analytical conclusions.*

---

**□ SCHEME SPECIFICITY PRESSURE TEST**
If I've classified a finding under a scheme category, can I name the specific variant, its characteristic evidentiary signature, and confirm my evidence matches that signature rather than a superficially similar pattern? *Failure mode: loose taxonomy that conflates structurally and legally distinct schemes, undermining the remediation and legal nexus analysis.*

---

**□ BENCHMARK ASSUMPTION TRANSPARENCY**
Have I stated explicitly what normalization assumptions underpin any comparative or ratio analysis? Have I acknowledged whether peer-company accounting policy differences could account for the observed variance without any misconduct? *Failure mode: benchmarking theater that produces statistically significant but analytically meaningless comparisons.*

---

**□ PERIOD SCOPE ADEQUACY**
Is the transaction window I examined sufficient to distinguish a scheme from a point anomaly? Have I verified whether extending the lookback period would materially change the pattern — and if I couldn't extend it, stated that limitation explicitly? *Failure mode: snapshot analysis that mischaracterizes isolated events as patterns, or mischaracterizes sustained schemes as isolated events.*

---

**□ RELATED-PARTY AND BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP COMPLETENESS**
In any finding involving inter-entity transactions, have I verified whether disclosed related parties exhaust the actual universe, or whether undisclosed related parties (identified through UCC filings, corporate registry cross-reference, payment flow analysis, or beneficial ownership tracing) exist that alter the economic substance analysis? *Failure mode: accepting the disclosed related-party schedule as authoritative when it is itself a potential instrument of concealment.*

---

**□ LEGAL NEXUS SPECIFICITY**
Have I mapped each significant finding to a specific statutory, regulatory, or professional standards provision rather than to a generic characterization? Have I been careful to distinguish criminal exposure (§ USC provisions, DOJ/FBI jurisdiction) from civil exposure (SEC enforcement, private right of action) from professional standards violations (GAAP departure, PCAOB deficiency)? *Failure mode: undifferentiated "violation" language that obscures the actual risk profile and misleads counsel on prosecutorial vs. regulatory vs. civil exposure.*

---

**□ SURVIVABILITY UNDER ADVERSARIAL REVIEW**
Would a qualified defense expert, given access to the same underlying data, find an equally or more parsimonious explanation for the anomalies I've identified? If yes, have I acknowledged that possibility and explained the evidentiary basis for my weighting? *Failure mode: producing a one-sided analytical narrative that a competent opposing expert can reframe without introducing new evidence.*

---

**□ SCOPE LIMITATION DISCLOSURE COMPLETENESS**
Have I explicitly stated what I did NOT have access to, what I did NOT test, what assumptions I was required to accept without verification, and how those limitations affect the reliability and completeness of my conclusions? *Failure mode: producing an apparently comprehensive analysis whose apparent comprehensiveness is itself misleading due to unstated scope constraints.*

---

**□ INDEPENDENCE AND OBJECTIVITY SELF-AUDIT**
Has the framing of the engagement, the fee structure, the client relationship, or the pressure of hypothesis confirmation introduced any directional bias into how evidence was selected, weighted, or presented? If I cannot affirmatively answer "no" to every vector of bias, have I disclosed it? *Failure mode: structural advocacy masquerading as independent forensic analysis — the failure mode that ends professional careers and invalidates litigation positions.*

---
